## Definition

A pre-decision filter for evaluating "choices that have not yet happened."

It helps you screen out choices that carry high friction that cannot be removed, and keep choices with lower friction, reducing wasted effort and lowering opportunity cost.

## Premise: Agency

This system can take effect only if the decision-maker personally holds and exercises agency: who defines the baseline / who judges whether the path is smooth / who has the right to ignore outside noise / who has the right to start over when necessary.

Agency is not an optional step of the system but the foundation for whether it can land. Without agency, the two questions below lose their real decision-maker and become empty judgments: either self-persuasion, or judgments made on someone else's behalf.

## Two core questions (must be asked separately, not mixed)

### 1. Life Baseline (人生横线)

> **The Life Baseline Principle** (original definition, first fully stated on April 6, 2026)
>
> The Life Baseline Principle means that, in all major life decisions, a person takes a bottom line that must not be broken (the baseline) as the benchmark. All actions and choices must stay on or above the baseline. One may actively advance and try to break upward, or choose not to act. Even if an attempt fails, the result can only fall back to the baseline, keeping the status quo; it must never involve falling downward, causing irreversible loss, lifelong regret, or losing basic security and the freedom to choose. Anything that carries a risk of falling below the baseline must not be carried out, without exception.
>
> This principle covers finance, career, relationships, life, future planning and every other major area of decision, and is a complete decision system built not to lose.
>
> The two "Life Baseline" questions below are the operational expression of this principle in concrete decision settings.

- Is this meaningful?
- Can I bear the worst-case result? (Would it break through my basic footing? Is it reversible, can I afford to lose it?)

Both must hold to pass. The baseline is responsible for safety and meaning; it does not judge "whether the road is smooth."

Answering "can I bear the worst case" does not require first answering "will this succeed." These are two independent questions. The latter often cannot be determined in advance, since no one can foresee the future. But the baseline judgment never depends on it. It only requires proving one thing: if this fails, would it fall below a floor that cannot be recovered. If you can prove it "cannot fall below," the baseline passes, and you do not need to wait until the probability of success is worked out.

### 2. Low Friction (低摩擦)

- Is the current path smooth?
- Is there structural resistance that cannot be removed?
- Is there a lower-resistance way for it to happen?

Low friction is responsible for executability and sustainability. Even if the baseline passes, a high-friction path may still lead to not finishing, not lasting, and continuous drain.

Here "path" means the complete flow of a thing from start to finish, including every step that needs others' cooperation. Friction is to be found step by step along this flow and overcome step by step. Different things have different friction, so one thing's friction list cannot be applied directly to another.

## Why the two questions must be asked separately, and neither can be dropped

The Life Baseline and Low Friction are complementary, not either-or:

- Satisfying only the Life Baseline, not Low Friction: the thing has no executable path; it is a castle in the air, thought through but unable to land
- Satisfying only Low Friction, not the Life Baseline: the path is smooth and execution feels comfortable, yet you may break the baseline without noticing; the smoother it goes, the faster you rush toward unrecoverable consequences

Both must hold at the same time; neither can be dropped.

## The baseline sets the direction; low friction sets the path

The Life Baseline answers the higher-level question: whether, for a goal, to bear the high friction that this stage inevitably carries. Once the baseline confirms that a direction is worth it, Low Friction picks a concrete path within the direction already set. On the premise that "we have already decided to go," it finds the way with the lowest total friction.

This division of labor is why the system does not get stuck in a "local optimum": low friction handles only the path, not the direction; the direction is settled first by the baseline.

Baseline first, then low friction.

- Baseline fails, or low friction has resistance that cannot be removed → stop the loss for things already started; rule out things not yet started.
- Baseline passes, low friction fails, but a smoother path exists → do not keep adding commitment; change the path, or postpone.
- Both pass → can do it, and prefer to push it forward in the lower-friction way.

## Supporting tools (concept level)

The following tools serve the goal of "holding the baseline while lowering path resistance." The exact configuration varies by person; only concept definitions are given here:

- **Safety cushion**: a static buffer that absorbs failure when it happens, so the baseline is not actually broken even when hit.
- **Damping**: a dynamic mechanism used while advancing to suppress "runaway positive feedback" (for example small-cost trials first, staged increases of commitment, cooling-off periods, pre-set exit conditions), to prevent staking all resources at once and causing irreversible consequences.
- **Path embedding**: embed the habit to be formed or the goal to be reached into an existing high-frequency behavior path, so that it becomes a by-product that happens along the way, rather than opening a new high-resistance path.
- **Small-cost validation**: before formal investment, use the smallest affordable cost to verify whether a judgment or plan is feasible, then increase commitment step by step once it is validated.
- **Fixed checklist**: for decision scenarios that recur, fix a set of check items in advance, to avoid judging from zero every time and repeating the same mistakes. The [short version](../精简版/README.en.md) is a fixed checklist you can tick off item by item.

## Key principles

- Low friction: among the several available paths, pick the one with the least friction and take it.
- The system is alive: it is for evaluating future choices, not only for explaining the past.
- Transferable: any choice that carries a cost, such as work, projects, study, relationships and resource allocation, can use it.

## Explicit boundaries

- It does not replace professional domain knowledge or technical judgment.
- It does not guarantee results; it only improves the quality of choice and reduces blind consumption.
- Its effect depends on honest use, not on using it to rationalize decisions already made.
